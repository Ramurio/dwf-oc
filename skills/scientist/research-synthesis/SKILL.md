# Research synthesis

Frame the user decision and its time window. Split the investigation by questions that can change the answer. Ask the foundation scout for original methods and the recent scout for later evidence under the same scope.

After scouting, distribute coverage across the researchers. Give each a question, a useful lead, a way to challenge the current explanation, and a description of the evidence to return.

Track material claims alongside their best source, measurement conditions, and uncertainty. Where findings conflict, compare populations, systems, metrics, and settings before deciding whether they actually disagree.

Use a further round for gaps that could change the answer. Tie committee feedback to concrete follow-up questions and preserve confirmed findings. Report which gaps remain, why they matter, and which conclusions are conditional.
